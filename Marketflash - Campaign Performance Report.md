**Marketflash \- Campaign Performance Report**

In 2023, MarketFlash ran one thousand campaigns — nearly three every single day — across five channels and eight markets worldwide, investing $24.9 million in the process. The year produced 509,878 conversions at an overall rate of 1.01% and a cost of $48.82 per customer. Those headline numbers tell a solid story. But they hide something important: performance varied enormously depending on where and how the budget was deployed, and the gap between our best and worst decisions was wide enough to matter significantly in 2024\.

So, let me tell you what we found in numbers and why it happened.

* TikTok and Email were our most efficient channels all year — both converted above average and both kept costs below $47 per conversion.   
* YouTube, on the other hand, ran 210 campaigns and returned the highest cost at $52.41 per conversion, nearly thirteen percent more expensive than TikTok, with a lower conversion rate to show for it. 

When we look at timing, it tells us a similar story: 

* March delivered our peak performance at 1.17% and $45.73 per conversion, while December fell to 0.88% and $57.58 — a thirty percent efficiency gap between our best and worst months, driven by the same budget. 

Among audiences, Male 18–40 converted best at 1.20%, while Seniors 60+ converted at just 0.79% for $61.30 per customer.And when we look at individual channel-audience combinations, the picture becomes even sharper: 

* Instagram targeting Adults 40–60 converted at 1.53%,   
* YouTube targeting Seniors 60+ converted at just 0.63% — costing $76.18 per conversion. We ran both. We should not have. 

Three structural problems explain most of this.

* The first is a mismatch between channel and audience intent — YouTube is an entertainment platform, not a purchasing one, and placing conversion-focused ads there for older audiences is simply the wrong combination of message, medium, and moment.   
* The second is seasonal misalignment: we concentrated spend in February and December, the two months when consumer intent is lowest and ad costs are highest.   
* The third is an uneven team: our top executive converted at 1.13%, our weakest at 0.96% — an eighteen percent gap that points directly to differences in brief quality and targeting strategy, not market conditions.

The patterns we saw in 2023 are structural, which means they will repeat in 2024 unless someone makes a deliberate choice to change them. YouTube's cost per conversion will keep drifting higher. February and December will keep delivering below-average results. The team gap will keep dragging on overall performance. None of this is inevitable — it is just what happens when no one acts. The encouraging part is that none of the fixes require more money. 

They require smarter allocation of what we already have: 

* Shifting twenty to thirty percent of YouTube spend to TikTok and Email could generate an estimated twelve to fifteen thousand additional conversions annually at the same total cost.   
* Stopping YouTube campaigns targeting Seniors 60+ immediately removes our single worst-performing combination from the budget.   
* Moving fifteen percent of February and December spend forward into March and the summer months — where we know conversion rates are strongest — recovers thousands of conversions from the same annual budget.   
* Scaling what already works, specifically Instagram and Email targeting the audiences that consistently convert above 1.4%, turns a proven result into a deliberate strategy rather than a lucky outcome. 

The data from this year has done something valuable: it has shown us exactly where the money is being well spent and exactly where it is not. The five hundred and nine thousand, eight hundred and seventy-eight conversions we delivered in 2023 are a genuine achievement. But with the right three decisions made this quarter, that number becomes five hundred and forty thousand and above — same team, same budget, better choices.
